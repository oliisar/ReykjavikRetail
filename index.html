Who We Are

Iceland's most trusted retail importer

Founded in the heart of Reykjavík, we have spent more than a decade building trade corridors between Iceland and the world. From fresh European foodstuffs to premium household goods, every product we import clears our stringent quality benchmarks before it reaches a single Icelandic shelf.

Beyond importing and distribution, we serve as trusted advisors to some of Iceland's most respected retail businesses — guiding strategy, supplier negotiations, and category management.

Common Questions

Before you ask

What product categories do you import? +
Over 200 product lines spanning food & beverage, personal care, household essentials and specialty goods — sourced directly from 80+ manufacturers in 25 countries, all vetted against Icelandic quality and compliance standards.
Do you deliver outside the capital region? +
Yes — our fleet of 40+ refrigerated and ambient vehicles runs scheduled routes to all 64 Icelandic municipalities, from the Westfjords to the East Fjords, with just-in-time delivery aligned to each retailer's inventory cycle.
How can our brand enter the Icelandic market? +
We act as the local partner for international brands: handling import compliance, listing negotiations with Icelandic retail chains, warehousing and nationwide distribution. Start with a Product Listing enquiry via the contact form and our procurement team will respond within two business days.
What does an advisory engagement look like? +
Engagements range from category strategy and feasibility studies to full M&A advisory. We have advised on the acquisition of some twenty Icelandic retail companies — around $100 million in combined transaction value — arranged financing, leases and property purchases, and led merger filings before the Icelandic Competition Authority.
Are you certified for food imports? +
Yes — our processes are EU HACCP and ISO 22000 compliant, certified by the Icelandic Food & Veterinary Authority (MAST), with live temperature monitoring across the full cold chain.
